---
title: "Night audit"
description: "Close the day: post room charges, flag no-shows, and move the business date forward."
---

The night audit is the hotel's end of day. It is the single most important
routine in the system, and it must run every night.

## What it does

Reading the audit step by step, it:

1. Makes sure the property has a business date.
2. Refuses to run twice for the same date at the same property.
3. Opens a bill for any in-house guest who somehow has none.
4. Posts **tonight's room charge** for every guest currently in house.
5. Flags every `Confirmed` reservation whose arrival date has passed as
   **`No Show`**, and charges the policy fee where one is set.
6. Clears waitlist entries more than two days past their requested departure.
7. Writes a **Night Audit Run** record: charges posted, amount, no-shows,
   folios opened, and a line-by-line log.
8. **Advances the business date** to the next day.

## Run it

**Billing → Night audit → Run**. It takes seconds on a small property, and
reports what it did:

```text
Night audit for 2026-09-21 complete.
Room charges posted   14   ₦1,190,000.00
No-shows flagged       2
Folios opened          0
Business date is now  2026-09-22
```

It can also run on a schedule, so a forgetful night can still be closed.

<Warning title="Run it every night">
Room charges post during the audit. Skip a night and the following morning
every in-house bill is short by a night's room charge, and guests check out
underpaying. Running it late is fine; not running it is not.
</Warning>

## Running it twice

Running it again for the same date does nothing and says so. That is
deliberate, so a nervous second attempt cannot double-charge every guest in
the hotel.

## The business date

The business date is the hotel's working day, and it moves **only** when the
audit runs. That is what keeps a 2 a.m. arrival attached to the night it
belongs to.

If revenue reports look a day out, check the business date first.

## Before you run it

- Post the day's restaurant and bar charges.
- Close cashier sessions and record variances.
- Check arrivals: anyone who arrived should be checked in, or they become a
  no-show.
