Cash is the part of hotel money most easily lost. Lodgiva tracks it by shift.

## The cashier session

A cashier session is one person's shift at one drawer. It records the opening
float, every cash movement during the shift, and the count at the end.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Open the session

At the start of your shift, open a session and enter the **opening float**:
the cash already in the drawer.

</Step>

<Step>

### Work the shift

Cash payments and cash refunds attach to your open session automatically. Petty
cash paid out is recorded as a voucher, with what it was for.

</Step>

<Step>

### Count and close

At the end of the shift, count the drawer and enter the figure. Lodgiva
compares it with what it expected and shows the variance.

</Step>

<Step>

### Hand over

The handover records what was passed to the next shift, and anything
outstanding they need to know.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Variances

A difference between counted and expected cash is a **variance**. Small ones
happen: change given wrongly, a note miscounted. Lodgiva records them rather
than hiding them, because the pattern matters more than any single night.

<Note>
A variance is not an accusation. It is a number that should be small and
occasionally non-zero. A drawer that balances perfectly every single night,
in cash, is worth a second look.
</Note>

## Cashier PIN

Sensitive operations — closing a bill into an invoice, cancelling an invoice —
can require a cashier PIN, separate from the sign-in password. It is set by
the cashier and reset by a manager.

## Petty cash

Money paid out of the drawer for small purchases is recorded as a petty cash
voucher with the reason, so the shift still balances at the end.
